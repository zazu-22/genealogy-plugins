# Research Logs

Maintaining documentation of the research process.

## Why Log Research

### Benefits of Good Logs

- **Avoid duplication**: Know what you've already searched
- **Track progress**: See what questions are answered
- **Document negative results**: Prove thoroughness
- **Enable continuation**: Pick up where you left off
- **Support conclusions**: Show the path to your findings

### The Cost of Poor Logging

- Repeat searches unknowingly
- Forget where information came from
- Can't verify sources later
- Can't prove exhaustive research
- Lose track of promising leads

## What to Log

### Every Search

At minimum, record:
- Date of search
- What you were looking for
- Where you searched
- What you found (or didn't find)

### Positive Results

- Full citation for source
- What information it contained
- Relevant to which research question
- Any notes on reliability or conflicts

### Negative Results

- What you searched
- Search parameters used
- Confirmation nothing was found
- Possible explanations or next steps

### Ideas and Leads

- Questions that arise during research
- Potential sources to check
- Connections noticed
- Theories to test

### Detailed Search Log

```
SEARCH LOG ENTRY

Date: 2024-01-15
Researcher: [Your name]

Research Question: When did John Smith die?

Search Performed:
  Repository: FamilySearch.org
  Collection: "Ohio, Deaths, 1908-1953"
  Search terms: "John Smith", Muskingum County
  Filters applied: Death year 1920-1930

Results:
  Status: [X] Found [ ] Not Found [ ] Partial

  Items Found:
    - Death certificate no. 12345 (1925), John W. Smith
      Digital image accessed: 2024-01-15
      Full citation recorded in source database

  Notes:
    - Confirmed death date as 15 March 1925
    - Informant was son Thomas Smith
    - Birthdate on cert: 15 March 1850 (secondary info)
    - Cause: heart failure

Follow-up needed:
  - Verify birth date independently
  - Find obituary for additional details
```

## Logging in Different Systems

### Paper-Based

**Advantages**:
- Simple, flexible
- Portable
- No technology required

**Organization**:
- One notebook per family line or project
- Index in front
- Date every entry

### Spreadsheet

**Advantages**:
- Searchable
- Sortable
- Easy to filter

**Setup**:
- Columns: Date, Question, Source, Result, Citation, Notes
- One row per search
- Filter by question or source

### Genealogy Software

**Gramps**:
- Use Notes for research logs
- Attach to Person or Family
- Use Research note type

**Logging alongside citations**:
- Source record for each source
- Citation for each use
- Notes for search details

### Dedicated Research Log Software

- Clooz
- RootsMagic research manager
- Custom database

## Best Practices

### Log Immediately

- Record searches as you do them
- Don't rely on memory
- Capture details while fresh

### Be Consistent

- Use same format for all logs
- Date every entry
- Include enough detail to repeat search

### Log Everything

- Successful searches (obviously)
- Unsuccessful searches (crucial for GPS)
- Partial results
- Interesting observations

### Review Regularly

- Check for overlooked leads
- Update question status
- Identify patterns

### Keep Logs with Project

- Link logs to relevant individuals/questions
- Back up regularly
- Make logs findable later

## Common Mistakes

### Insufficient Detail

**Problem**: "Searched census - not found"
**Better**: "Searched 1850 U.S. Census, Muskingum County, Ohio for 'John Smith' and variations 'Smyth', 'Schmidt' - no matching entry found among 23 Smith families in county"

### No Negative Logging

**Problem**: Only logging when you find something
**Impact**: Can't prove exhaustive research
**Fix**: Log every search, especially negatives

### Delayed Logging

**Problem**: Logging at end of day or later
**Impact**: Details forgotten, searches missed
**Fix**: Log in real-time or immediately after

### Scattered Logs

**Problem**: Logs in multiple places
**Impact**: Can't find previous research
**Fix**: Centralize logging system
