# Locality Research

Understanding record creation, survival, and location by jurisdiction.

## Why Locality Matters

### Records Depend on Location

- Different places created different records
- Boundaries change over time
- Record survival varies
- Repositories differ by jurisdiction

### The Key Question

> "What records were created, and where are they now?"

## Understanding Jurisdictions

### U.S. Record Hierarchy

```
Federal
  └── State/Territory
       └── County (or equivalent)
            └── Township/Municipality
                 └── Local entities (church, cemetery, etc.)
```

### Who Created What

| Record Type | Primary Creator |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Census | Federal government |
| Military service | Federal government |
| Immigration/Naturalization | Federal/Local courts |
| Vital records | State or county (varies) |
| Land records | State/Federal (early), County (later) |
| Probate | County court |
| Court records | County/State courts |
| Tax records | County/State |
| Church records | Religious bodies |

### Boundary Changes

**Counties split**: Old records in parent county
**Counties merged**: May be in successor county
**States created**: Territory records may be elsewhere
**Cities annex**: Records may be in different locations

**Always verify**: What was the jurisdiction at the time of the event?

## Research Workflow by Location

### Step 1: Identify the Location

- Where did the event occur?
- What jurisdiction applied at that time?
- Have boundaries changed?

### Step 2: Identify Available Records

- What records were created there then?
- Have the records survived?
- Where are they now?

### Step 3: Locate Repositories

**Primary repositories**:
- County courthouse
- State archives
- National Archives (federal records)

**Secondary access**:
- FamilySearch (microfilm, digital)
- Ancestry, FindMyPast, MyHeritage
- Local libraries and historical societies

### Step 4: Access Records

**In person**:
- Call ahead for hours and procedures
- Know what you're looking for
- Bring research notes

**Online**:
- Check FamilySearch catalog
- Check commercial databases
- Check state/county websites

**By mail**:
- Use proper request forms
- Include payment if required
- Allow processing time

## Dealing with Record Losses

### Common Causes

| Cause | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| Courthouse fire | Major loss of county records |
| Flood/disaster | May affect specific record types |
| War | Loss or displacement |
| Neglect | Deterioration over time |
| Never created | Some records never existed |

### When Records Are Lost

1. **Confirm the loss**: Check multiple sources
2. **Identify what survives**: Often something remains
3. **Find substitutes**:
   - Church records for vital events
   - Tax/land records for residence
   - Newspaper for events
   - Census for family structure
4. **Check for copies**:
   - State copies of local records
   - Church headquarters records
   - DAR/lineage society compilations

### Substitute Records

| Lost Record | Possible Substitutes |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Birth | Baptism, census age, delayed registration |
| Marriage | Church record, newspaper, census |
| Death | Church burial, newspaper, probate |
| Land | Tax lists, maps, surveys |
| Probate | Land records, pension files |

## Locality Checklist

### Questions to Answer

1. What county (or equivalent) applies?
2. Has this county always existed?
3. What records were created here?
4. Where are those records now?
5. What's available online?
6. What requires in-person or mail research?

## Tips for Unfamiliar Locations

### Starting Research in New Area

1. **Read the FamilySearch Wiki** for that location
2. **Check for research guides** (county, state)
3. **Join local society** (even remotely, for publications)
4. **Find a local researcher** if needed
5. **Start with online resources** before visiting
6. **Contact repositories** before traveling

### Using Local Resources

**Historical societies**:
- May have unique collections
- Often have knowledgeable volunteers
- May offer research assistance

**Local libraries**:
- Local history collections
- Newspaper archives
- City directories

**Genealogical societies**:
- Query columns
- Surname indexes
- Local expertise
