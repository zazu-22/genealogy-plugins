# DNA Test Types

Overview of different DNA test types and their genealogical applications.

## Autosomal DNA (atDNA)

### What It Is

Autosomal DNA is the DNA from your 22 pairs of non-sex chromosomes. You inherit roughly 50% from each parent, who inherited from their parents, and so on.

### Inheritance Pattern

```
You
├── 50% from Mother
│   ├── ~25% from Maternal Grandmother
│   └── ~25% from Maternal Grandfather
└── 50% from Father
    ├── ~25% from Paternal Grandmother
    └── ~25% from Paternal Grandfather
```

**Key point**: Each generation roughly halves the DNA inherited from earlier ancestors.

### What It Can Tell You

- Biological relatives within ~6 generations
- Ethnicity estimates (with limitations)
- Confirmation of documented relationships
- Unexpected relationships

### Limitations

- Cannot distinguish maternal from paternal matches without additional testing/triangulation
- Amount shared varies—siblings don't share exactly 50%
- Distant relationships may share no detectable DNA
- Ethnicity estimates are probabilistic, not definitive

### Best For

- Finding cousins and close relatives
- Confirming recent ancestry (grandparents, great-grandparents)
- General genetic genealogy

## Y-DNA

### What It Is

Y-DNA is the DNA on the Y chromosome, which only males have. It passes virtually unchanged from father to son.

### Inheritance Pattern

```
You (if male)
└── Father
    └── Paternal Grandfather
        └── Paternal Great-Grandfather
            └── [Direct paternal line continues]
```

### What It Can Tell You

- Direct paternal line ancestry
- Surname connections (Y-DNA follows surnames in patrilineal cultures)
- Deep ancestry (haplogroups)
- Whether two males share a common paternal ancestor

### Limitations

- Only available to males (females need a male relative to test)
- Only traces one line (direct paternal)
- Mutations can cause differences even between close relatives

### Best For

- Confirming paternal lineage
- Surname studies
- Connecting to a specific male line ancestor
- Identifying biological father (with other male line relatives to compare)

## Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)

### What It Is

Mitochondrial DNA is found in the mitochondria (cell energy producers). It passes from mother to all children, but only daughters pass it on.

### Inheritance Pattern

```
You
└── Mother
    └── Maternal Grandmother
        └── Maternal Great-Grandmother
            └── [Direct maternal line continues]
```

### What It Can Tell You

- Direct maternal line ancestry
- Deep ancestry (haplogroups)
- Whether two people share a common maternal ancestor

### Limitations

- Mutates slowly—matches may be thousands of years apart
- Only traces one line (direct maternal)
- More limited for genealogical timeframes than atDNA

### Best For

- Confirming maternal lineage
- Deep ancestry questions
- When maternal line is the only option

## X-DNA

### What It Is

X-DNA is from the X chromosome. Males have one X (from mother), females have two (one from each parent).

### Inheritance Pattern

Unique pattern:
- Males get X only from mother
- Females get X from both parents
- Creates predictable patterns across generations

### What It Can Tell You

- Can help narrow which lines a match is from
- Follows specific inheritance paths

### Limitations

- Complex inheritance patterns
- Not all testing companies provide X-DNA data
- Requires understanding of X-DNA inheritance charts

### Best For

- Supplementing atDNA analysis
- Narrowing down possible relationship paths

## Choosing a Test

### Start Here: Autosomal (atDNA)

**Recommended for most genealogists because**:
- Tests all ancestral lines
- Finds the most matches
- Most useful for typical genealogy timeframes

### Add Y-DNA When

- You have a specific paternal line question
- You're male and want to connect surname lines
- You need to distinguish between paternal line candidates

### Add mtDNA When

- You have a specific maternal line question
- atDNA hasn't resolved a maternal line mystery
- Deep maternal ancestry is of interest

## Testing Considerations

### Who to Test

- Test oldest generation available (more DNA from target ancestors)
- Test multiple relatives to cover more lines
- Test both sides of the family if possible

### Timing

- Test elderly relatives first (before opportunity is lost)
- DNA doesn't change—your test will always be valid

### Privacy

- Consider privacy implications
- Understand each company's policies
- DNA can reveal unexpected relationships

## Interpreting Results

### Ethnicity Estimates

- Estimates, not definitive facts
- Vary between companies
- Reflect reference populations, not historical nations
- Most useful for continental-level origins

### Match Lists

- Sorted by amount of shared DNA
- Include predicted relationships
- Require analysis to determine actual connection

### Raw Data

- Can be downloaded from most companies
- Allows upload to third-party tools
- Useful for in-depth analysis
