# Information Types

Classifying information based on the knowledge and reliability of the informant.

## The Critical Question

> "Who provided this information, and what did they know?"

The same source can contain both primary and secondary information for different facts.

## Primary Information

### Definition
Information provided by someone with **firsthand knowledge** of the event.

### Characteristics
- Informant personally witnessed or participated
- Recorded at or near the time of the event
- Based on direct observation, not hearsay

### Classic Examples

| Event | Primary Informant | Why Primary |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| Birth date | Mother | Participated in event |
| Baptism | Minister | Performed ceremony |
| Marriage | Bride/Groom | Participated |
| Death date | Attending physician | Was present |
| Burial location | Sexton | Conducted burial |

### Limits of Primary Information

**Primary does not mean accurate.**

Even eyewitnesses can:
- Misremember
- Be confused
- Provide information with bias
- Make errors in recording

**Example**: A mother reporting her child's birth date may misremember if recorded years later (still primary, but less reliable).

## Secondary Information

### Definition
Information provided by someone **not directly involved** who learned it from others.

### Characteristics
- Informant learned information secondhand
- May be reported years after the event
- Based on what someone else said

### Classic Examples

| Event | Secondary Informant | Why Secondary |
|-------|---------------------|---------------|
| Birth date | Death certificate (funeral director) | Learned from family |
| Father's birthplace | Son on census | Told by father/others |
| Marriage date | Widow on pension application | May be recalling |
| Parent's death | Child reporting years later | Was told, didn't witness |

### The Death Certificate Problem

Death certificates often contain:

| Information | Usually Primary | Usually Secondary |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Date of death | ✓ (attending physician) | |
| Cause of death | ✓ (attending physician) | |
| Burial place | ✓ (funeral director) | |
| Date of birth | | ✓ (family member) |
| Birthplace | | ✓ (family member) |
| Parent names | | ✓ (family member) |
| Spouse name | | ✓ (family member) |

**Always identify who provided what information on the document.**

## Indeterminate Information

### Definition
Information where the **informant cannot be identified** or their knowledge level is unclear.

### Common Situations

| Situation | Why Indeterminate |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Unsigned document | No identified informant |
| Multiple possible informants | Cannot distinguish who provided what |
| Unknown creation circumstances | Cannot assess informant's knowledge |
| Compiled records | Original informant obscured |

### Examples
- Early census records with no identified enumerator source
- Index entries without underlying record access
- Family Bible with unknown record-keeper
- Newspaper articles without named sources

### Handling Indeterminate Information
1. Acknowledge the limitation
2. Look for corroborating evidence
3. Note it in your analysis
4. Don't give it the same weight as primary information

## Mixed Information Sources

### Common Documents with Mixed Types

#### Birth Certificates
| Field | Information Type | Why |
|-------|-----------------|-----|
| Child's name | Primary | Parent naming child |
| Birth date | Primary | Mother/midwife present |
| Birth place | Primary | Event location |
| Father's age | Variable | Father may self-report (primary) or be estimated |
| Father's birthplace | Secondary | Child born later, father reporting his own origin |
| Mother's age | Primary | Self-reported |
| Mother's birthplace | Secondary | Typically reported from her account |

#### Census Records
| Field | Information Type | Why |
|-------|-----------------|-----|
| Names in household | Primary | Self-reported by head |
| Relationships | Primary | Head identifying relationships |
| Ages | Variable | Self-reported but often estimated |
| Birthplaces | Secondary for children | Parents reporting |
| Occupations | Primary | Self-reported |
| Property value | Primary/Secondary | May be self-reported or assessed |

#### Marriage Records
| Field | Information Type | Why |
|-------|-----------------|-----|
| Marriage date | Primary | Officiant recording |
| Names of couple | Primary | Self-identification |
| Ages of couple | Primary | Self-reported (may be inaccurate) |
| Parents' names | Primary | Self-reported |
| Witnesses | Primary | Self-identification |
| Previous marriages | Primary | Self-reported |

## Evaluating Informant Reliability

### Factors Favoring Reliability
- Event recorded close in time
- Informant had direct knowledge
- No motivation to falsify
- Consistent with other records
- Official capacity to record

### Factors Suggesting Caution
- Years between event and recording
- Information from hearsay chain
- Potential motivation to misstate
- Conflicts with other evidence
- Informal or non-official record

## Practical Application

### Analysis Workflow

1. **Identify the document**
   - What kind of record is this?
   - When was it created?

2. **Identify the informant(s)**
   - Who provided the information?
   - Is this stated or inferred?

3. **Classify each piece of information**
   - Primary: They were there
   - Secondary: They heard about it
   - Indeterminate: Can't determine

4. **Assess reliability**
   - How close in time?
   - What motivation exists?
   - How consistent with other sources?

### Recording in Research

**Example notation**:
```
Death Certificate of John Smith (1950)
- Death date (12 Oct 1950): PRIMARY - physician present
- Death place (County Hospital): PRIMARY - physician recorded
- Birth date (15 Mar 1877): SECONDARY - son as informant
- Father's name (William Smith): SECONDARY - son as informant
- Mother's name (Mary Jones): SECONDARY - son as informant
```

## The Genealogical Pitfall

**Never assume a record is entirely primary or secondary.**

Each fact within a document may have a different information type based on:
- Who provided that specific fact
- When they provided it
- What they could have known

**Best practice**: Analyze information types for each significant fact, not just for the document as a whole.
