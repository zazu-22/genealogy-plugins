# Evidence Types

Understanding how information serves as evidence for genealogical questions.

## The Key Distinction

**Evidence is not the same as information.**

- **Information**: What a source tells us
- **Evidence**: How that information relates to our specific question

The same piece of information can serve as different types of evidence depending on the question being asked.

## Direct Evidence

### Definition
Evidence that **explicitly answers** the research question without requiring interpretation or additional evidence.

### Characteristics
- States the answer directly
- Does not require inference
- Stands alone to answer the question

### Examples

| Question | Direct Evidence |
|----------|-----------------|
| When was John born? | Birth certificate stating "15 March 1850" |
| Who were Mary's parents? | Baptism record naming parents |
| Where did they marry? | Marriage certificate naming location |
| What was his occupation? | Census listing "blacksmith" |

### Strength of Direct Evidence

Direct evidence is **not automatically correct.**

Consider:

- Source quality (original vs. derivative)
- Information quality (primary vs. secondary)
- Potential for error or falsification

**Example**: A death certificate may directly state birthdate, but that information is typically secondary (provided by family member, not by someone present at birth).

## Indirect Evidence

### Definition
Evidence that **implies or suggests** an answer but requires interpretation, inference, or combination with other evidence.

### Characteristics
- Does not state the answer explicitly
- Requires reasoning to reach conclusion
- Often needs corroboration
- May point to multiple possibilities

### Common Forms

#### Age at Event → Birth Year
| Event Record | Information | Inference |
|--------------|-------------|-----------|
| 1850 Census | "Age 45" | Born ca. 1805 |
| Marriage 1875 | "Age 21" | Born ca. 1854 |
| Death 1920 | "Age 68" | Born ca. 1852 |

**Note**: Calculate ranges, not exact dates:

- Age 45 in June 1850 → Born June 1804 to June 1805

#### Naming Patterns → Parentage
- Children often named for grandparents
- Middle names may preserve maiden names
- But naming patterns are **cultural, not universal**

#### Geographic Proximity → Relationships
- Families appearing near each other in records
- Sequential land purchases
- Witness relationships

#### Economic Evidence → Life Events
- Sale of property → possible death, migration, or financial trouble
- Guardian appointment → parent deceased
- Estate inventory → death followed by settlement

### Building a Case with Indirect Evidence

Multiple pieces of indirect evidence can establish proof when:

1. Each piece is independently sourced
2. Evidence points consistently to same conclusion
3. Alternative explanations are considered and eliminated
4. The cumulative weight is compelling

## Negative Evidence

### Definition
Evidence derived from the **absence of information** where it would reasonably be expected.

### Requirements for Valid Negative Evidence
1. You searched thoroughly in the expected source
2. The source is reasonably complete for that time/place
3. The person would likely appear if present
4. The absence is meaningful

### Examples

| Search | Finding | Possible Inference |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| 1850 Census of County A | Family not found | May have lived elsewhere |
| Tax list 1820 | Name absent | May have died, moved, or not owned taxable property |
| Church records 1840-1860 | No baptism | May belong to different church |
| Probate index | No estate file | May have died without probate assets |

### Using Negative Evidence

**Strong negative evidence**:

- Comprehensive, well-preserved records
- Clear expectation person would appear
- No alternative explanation for absence

**Weak negative evidence**:

- Incomplete records
- Record survival issues
- Multiple possible explanations

### Common Applications

#### Mortality
- Appears in 1870 census, absent from 1880 → likely died 1870-1880
- Named in father's 1835 will, not in 1840 tax → may have died

#### Migration
- Present in records through 1820, absent after → may have moved
- Combined with appearance in new location → confirms migration

#### Identity Elimination
- Searching for "John Smith" born ca. 1800
- Found one "John Smith" who died 1810 → eliminate as candidate
