# Reasonably Exhaustive Research

GPS Element 1: Conducting thorough research across all relevant sources.

## What "Reasonably Exhaustive" Means

### The Standard

Research is "reasonably exhaustive" when you have:

- Searched all sources likely to provide information
- Extended research to related persons and places
- Documented searches that yielded no results
- Continued until evidence points clearly to a conclusion

### What It Doesn't Mean

- **Not infinite**: You don't need to search every possible source
- **Not perfect**: Some records may be unavailable or destroyed
- **Not static**: New sources may emerge; revisit if they do

## Determining Scope

### Questions to Guide Scope

1. **What is the research question?**
   - Specific question defines what's relevant
   - Different questions require different sources

2. **What time period is involved?**
   - Records available vary by era
   - Earlier periods have fewer record types

3. **What jurisdiction applies?**
   - Federal, state, county, local records
   - Religious denomination records

4. **What record types exist for this time/place?**
   - Not all records survived
   - Some were never created

### Scope by Question Type

| Question | Minimum Source Types |
|----------|---------------------|
| Birth date | Vital records, church records, census, family sources |
| Parentage | Vital records, census, probate, church, DNA |
| Marriage | Vital records, church, newspaper, census, bible |
| Death | Vital records, cemetery, newspaper, probate, census |
| Identity | All available for time/place |

**Note:** For a comprehensive list of record types, query the research database's `record_types` table, which is the authoritative registry for this project.

## Extending Research

### FAN Club Principle

Research the **Family, Associates, and Neighbors**:

**Family**: Extended family may hold clues

- Siblings and their records
- Aunts, uncles, cousins
- In-laws

**Associates**: People connected through activity

- Business partners
- Church members
- Organization members

**Neighbors**: Proximity often means connection

- Census neighbors
- Land record neighbors
- Witnesses on documents

### Cluster Research

Study the entire cluster of related individuals:

- Multiple records may connect
- One person's record may name another
- Patterns emerge from aggregate

## Documenting Negative Searches

### Why Document Negatives

- Proves thoroughness
- Prevents duplicate effort
- Identifies gaps for future research
- Supports negative evidence claims

### What to Record

```
Search Documentation

Date: [search date]
Repository: [where searched]
Record type: [what type of record]
Search parameters: [names, dates, locations searched]
Results: [what was found or not found]
Notes: [limitations, gaps, observations]
```

### Example

```
Date: 15 January 2024
Repository: FamilySearch, "Ohio, Deaths, 1908-1953"
Record type: Death certificates
Search parameters: "John Smith" died 1920-1930, Muskingum County
Results: No matching record found
Notes: Index may be incomplete; check county records directly
```

## Assessing Completeness

### Before Concluding, Ask:

1. **Sources searched**: Have I checked all available record types?
2. **Repositories checked**: Have I searched multiple repositories?
3. **Variants tried**: Have I searched name variants?
4. **Related persons**: Have I researched the FAN club?
5. **Negatives documented**: Have I recorded what I didn't find?

### Indicators of Sufficient Research

**Strong indicators**:

- Multiple independent sources agree
- All obvious record types searched
- No significant gaps in timeline
- Research question clearly answered

**Warning signs**:

- Only one source type searched
- Major record category unexplored
- Conflicting evidence not investigated
- Obvious leads not followed

## Practical Limitations

### When to Stop

You can reasonably conclude research when:

- Available sources have been searched
- Additional searching yields diminishing returns
- Evidence clearly supports a conclusion
- Remaining sources unlikely to change outcome

### Documenting Limitations

Be transparent about:

- Records that don't survive
- Repositories not accessible
- Languages you couldn't read
- Time/resource constraints

### Example Limitation Statement

```
Research was conducted in available records for Muskingum County,
Ohio, 1850-1900. The following limitations apply:
- County courthouse fire (1887) destroyed some early records
- Church records for [denomination] not located
- German-language records not fully translated

Despite these limitations, the available evidence supports...
```

## Common Mistakes

### Insufficient Research

- Stopping at first source found
- Ignoring difficult-to-access records
- Not checking variant spellings
- Overlooking related persons

### Over-Research

- Searching irrelevant records
- Continuing despite clear conclusion
- Delaying conclusions indefinitely
- Perfectionism preventing progress

### Poor Documentation

- Not recording searches
- Forgetting negative results
- Unclear notes on what was searched
- No record of repositories checked
